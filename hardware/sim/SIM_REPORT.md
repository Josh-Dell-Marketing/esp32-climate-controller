# Relay carrier PCB: circuit simulation report

Generated by `run_sim.py` on 2026-11-13 with ngspice (KiCad 10.0.8 `ngspice.dll`). Re-run with `.venv\Scripts\python.exe run_sim.py` from this folder. Every number below comes from that run, except test 5, which is a hand calculation in the same script.

## Verdict

The relay driver design is sound. Base drive, saturation, coil voltage at 4.5 V, the flyback clamp, the LED and transistor dissipation all pass with large margin. Nothing in the driver depends on the unknown coil inductance; the inductance changes only the timing.

Three things need a decision before ordering, and one needs documenting:

1. **Power path headroom for the NodeMCU's AMS1117 (FAIL at the edges).** The 1N5819 plus polyfuse drop leaves the +5V rail at 4.47 V minimum with a 5.00 V supply and a typical diode (only 70 mV above the 4.4 V the AMS1117 needs). It falls to 4.32 V with a diode at its datasheet-max Vf, and to 4.23-4.07 V with a 4.75 V supply. The relays are not affected; they only need 3.75 V. In dropout the AMS1117 output sags to about rail - 1.1 V (down to ~2.97 V). The ESP8266 is specified down to 2.5 V, so it will probably keep running, but there is no margin. **Fix, choose one:**
   - **A (recommended):** replace D_REV with an ideal-diode IC that blocks reverse current, e.g. TI **LM66100** (SC-70-6, 1.5-5.5 V, 1.5 A, 79 mohm typ, reverse-current blocking, reverse-polarity standoff to -6 V). Simulated as an 80 mohm path: rail minimum 4.55 V even at a 4.75 V input. Do **not** use a plain P-MOSFET with its gate tied to GND: it conducts both ways, so with the NodeMCU on USB it would back-feed the input terminal. The 1N5819 and LM66100 do not.
   - **B:** keep the 1N5819 and specify the supply as 5.1-5.25 V (most DIN-rail and open-frame 5 V supplies have a trim pot). At 5.25 V with a datasheet-max diode the rail minimum is 4.56 V.
   - C: a lower-Vf Schottky (3-5 A part, e.g. 1N5822 / SS54 / a Nexperia PMEG low-VF part) gains roughly 0.05-0.15 V at 0.4 A. That helps but does not fix the 4.75 V case on its own.
2. **Boot with an internal pull-up enabled (FAIL with Rpd = 10 k).** If GPIO12/13/14 come up with the internal ~30 k pull-up enabled, the 30 k / (1 k + 10 k) divider puts 0.65 V on the base. Q1 then conducts 8.5 mA and the coil sees 592 mV, above the 0.5 V must-release level. The relay will not click (it needs 3.75 V), but it is not "fully off". A 50 k pull-up gives 105 uA (marginal). The ESP8266 does not normally enable these pull-ups unless firmware sets INPUT_PULLUP, so in practice this is a robustness margin. **Fix: change Rpd from 10 k to 4.7 k.** Q1 then stays fully off (Vbe <= 0.43 V, Ic ~2 uA) with a 30 k pull-up. Drive stays fine at the worst case (4.5 V, hFE ~250 model, 40 ohm GPIO): coil 4.45 V, Vce(sat) 53 mV, GPIO current ~2.4 mA.
3. **I2C at 400 pF fails at 100 kHz; 400 kHz only works for very short cables.** A 0.5-1 m cable plus the SHT31 and the ESP8266 pins is typically 80-150 pF. That passes at 100 kHz with 4.7 k alone (< ~250 pF) or 4.7 k || 10 k (< ~370 pF). **Run the bus at 100 kHz.** If the cable is long or shielded (higher pF), fit **2.2 k** pull-ups on the carrier: 611 ns at 400 pF, with sink current 1.6 mA, still inside 3 mA.
4. **Contact traces: document 8 A per channel** (conservative IPC-2221 limit for a 20 C rise is 8.9 A). 10 A gives about 26 C rise on the conservative method. See test 5.

Also worth knowing:

- USB-only power works: relays pull in (rail >= 4.24 V at a 4.75 V USB port) and the 1N5819 blocks back-feed into the input terminal (~55 uA model leakage, even into a 1 ohm dead supply). The AMS1117 headroom is marginal on a 4.75 V port, as in point 1. Four relays plus the NodeMCU draw 392 mA average and 566 mA peak through the USB port and the NodeMCU's onboard USB diode. That is over a USB 2.0 port's 500 mA during WiFi bursts. Check the diode fitted on your NodeMCU (clones vary) and avoid running all four relays from a laptop USB port for long.
- GPIO16 boot glitch (fan channel): a 50 ms high at boot pulls the relay in after 3.0 ms; it releases 3.3 ms after the pin goes low (L = 150 mH). The fan relay will click on briefly at every boot. That is accepted; document it.

## Notes per test

### 1. Relay driver (`test1_relay_driver.cir`, plots `test1_*.png`)

- The base gets 2.36 mA ((3.3 - 0.75) / 1 k, minus 75 uA into Rpd). With ~71 mA collector current that is a forced beta of ~30, far below the BC337-40's minimum hFE of 250, so Q1 is hard in saturation: Vce(sat) 55 mV. Even the low-gain model at 4.5 V gives forced beta 27. 1 k is a good value; no change needed.
- Coil voltage when on is supply minus ~55 mV: 4.94 V at 5.0 V and 4.45 V at 4.5 V (must-operate 3.75 V). The relays will still pull in down to a rail of about 3.8 V.
- Turn-off with the 1N4148: the collector clamps at 5.82 V (supply + ~0.8 V). The diode carries the coil current (71 mA peak, decaying over a few ms), well within the 1N4148's 200 mA average rating and 1 A / 1 ms surge rating.
- Turn-off without the diode: nothing limits the collector except Q1's own breakdown. In the model with no breakdown it rings up to 4.2 kV. With the BC337's V(BR)CES = 50 V modelled, Q1 avalanches at ~50 V (above Vceo 45 V) and absorbs the coil energy (up to 1.29 mJ per switch-off at 500 mH). The BC337 has no avalanche energy rating. The LED also sees 45 V reverse (rated 5 V). The 1N4148 is required.
- Coil inductance sweep (50 / 150 / 500 mH): all steady-state values (base current, Vce(sat), coil voltage, LED current, clamp voltage, diode peak current) are identical across the sweep. Only the timing scales with L/R:
  - L = 50mH: coil current reaches the must-operate level 1.0 ms after the GPIO goes high; after the GPIO goes low it falls below 30 % in 0.7 ms and below the 10 % must-release level in 1.1 ms.
  - L = 150mH: coil current reaches the must-operate level 3.0 ms after the GPIO goes high; after the GPIO goes low it falls below 30 % in 2.0 ms and below the 10 % must-release level in 3.3 ms.
  - L = 500mH: coil current reaches the must-operate level 10.1 ms after the GPIO goes high; after the GPIO goes low it falls below 30 % in 6.6 ms and below the 10 % must-release level in 10.9 ms.
  The real release delay is the decay time plus the armature's mechanical release (datasheet: release <= 5 ms). A diode-only clamp makes the release slower than a zener clamp would, which is normal and harmless here. Only consider a zener + diode clamp if fast release ever matters.
- LED current 3.0 mA at 5.0 V (2.5 mA at 4.5 V), as expected. It is carried by Q1 in addition to the coil.
- Q1 dissipation is 5.9 mW when on, against 625 mW for the TO-92. The 150 mW peak lasts well under a microsecond, during the switching edge.

### 2. Boot (`test2_boot.cir`, plot `test2_gpio16_boot_pulse.png`)

- Open (hi-Z) pin: Rpd holds the base at 0 V. Relay off, Ic = 0. PASS.
- 30 uA leakage into the pin: 30 uA x 10 k = 0.3 V on the base. Q1 off. PASS.
- Internal pull-up 30-50 k: see the Verdict, point 2. Change Rpd to 4.7 k.
- GPIO16 50 ms high at boot: the relay pulls in. Accepted for the fan; document it.

### 3. Power path (`test3_power_path.cir`, plot `test3_rail_droop.png`)

- Stimulus: all four relays switch on together at 10 ms. The NodeMCU draws 80 mA with 300 mA WiFi bursts (2 ms every 10 ms), the first one exactly at the relay switch-on. This is harsher than real WiFi traffic.
- Voltage budget at a 5.00 V input, all relays on, between bursts (~347 mA): lead ~7 mV, polyfuse ~87 mV (0.25 ohm), 1N5819 348 mV typical / 492 mV datasheet-max. Rail 4.56 V / 4.42 V. A 300 mA burst takes a further ~0.1 V. The 470 uF capacitor smooths the 10 us edges, but a 2 ms burst is too long for it to carry.
- The polyfuse resistance is the brief's 0.25 ohm. Check the MF-R110's R1max (resistance after it has tripped once). Each extra 0.1 ohm costs about 40 mV at 0.4 A.
- Total current: 347 mA with all relays on and the NodeMCU at 80 mA; 392 mA average including bursts; 565 mA peak. That is well inside F1's 1.1 A hold current, which derates to roughly 0.9 A at 50 C ambient. F1 will not nuisance-trip.
- 1N5819 dissipation 139-193 mW. Fine for a DO-41 part.
- The Diodes Inc DI_1N5819 model is shown for reference only. It gives ~0.76 V at 1 A, which is beyond the 0.60 V datasheet maximum, so it overstates the drop.
- USB-only mode: modelled as the USB port -> 0.25 ohm cable -> the NodeMCU's series Schottky (~0.35 V at 0.4 A) -> +5V. The input terminal is either open or loaded by 1 ohm (a dead supply). The 1N5819's reverse leakage is the only back-feed path.
- Fix A (LM66100-class ideal diode) is modelled only as its forward on-resistance. Its reverse-current blocking (internal comparator) and -6 V reverse-polarity standoff are from the TI datasheet and are not simulated. Check its 5.5 V maximum input against your supply's worst-case overshoot; a reversed 5 V supply (-5 V) is inside its -6 V rating with little margin.

### 4. I2C (`test4_i2c_rise.cir`, plot `test4_i2c_rise.png`)

- The rise time follows 0.847 x R x C, as the simulation confirms. Limits: 1000 ns for 100 kHz, 300 ns for 400 kHz (I2C spec, 30-70 %).
- Sink current at VOL = 0.4 V: 0.62 mA (4.7 k) and 0.91 mA (4.7 k || 10 k), far below the 3 mA limit. You could go as low as ~1 k total pull-up before reaching 3 mA.
- Recommendation: 100 kHz in firmware. Keep 4.7 k on the carrier, and leave the footprint so 2.2 k can be fitted for a long or shielded cable.

### 5. Relay contact traces (hand calculation, IPC-2221)

- Method: the IPC-2221 external-layer formula I = 0.048 x dT^0.44 x A^0.725 (A in mil^2). One trace, 2.0 mm x 35 um = 78.7 mil x 1.38 mil = 109 mil^2. IPC-2152 was not used: it is chart-based and needs board-thickness and plane modifiers we do not have yet. For a single external trace on 1.6 mm FR4 with no planes it gives results of the same order.
- Top and bottom copies run directly over each other on a 1.6 mm board, so they heat each other. Two bounds are given. **Conservative:** one conductor of twice the area (2 x 108.5 mil^2). **Optimistic:** each layer carries half the current and cools on its own. Use the conservative figure.

| Current | dT conservative | dT optimistic | Drop (25 mm, both layers) | Power in trace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 A | 1.7 C | 1.1 C | 10 mV | 30 mW |
| 5 A | 5.4 C | 3.5 C | 17 mV | 83 mW |
| 8 A | 15.8 C | 10.3 C | 27 mV | 212 mW |
| 10 A | 26.3 C | 17.1 C | 33 mV | 331 mW |

- Max current for a 20 C rise: **8.9 A conservative**, 10.7 A optimistic. Trace resistance is 6.6 mohm per layer (3.3 mohm both layers, copper at 40 C).
- **Document a per-channel limit of 8 A continuous (resistive).** Other parts of the path may set a lower limit. The SRD-05VDC-SL-C is rated 10 A at 250 VAC / 30 VDC resistive, and much less for motor or inductive loads; check its datasheet derating. The screw terminal block's rating and the relay pins themselves may also be lower. Both trace ends land on through-hole pads, which already join the top and bottom copper; a few stitching vias along the 25 mm run do no harm.

## Assumptions

- **Coil inductance:** not in the Songle datasheet. 150 mH is the nominal value. Bench measurements of this relay family are roughly 0.1-0.2 H, and L/R ~2 ms fits the 10 ms operate / 5 ms release spec. Results are reported for 50, 150 and 500 mH; only the timing changes. Coil inter-winding capacitance is assumed at 20 pF (it matters only without the diode). The coil is 70 ohm.
- **ESP8266 GPIO:** 3.3 V push-pull with 30 ohm output resistance (40 ohm in the worst-case run). Internal pull-up 30-50 k. Leakage 30 uA.
- **NodeMCU load:** an ideal current sink, 80 mA plus 300 mA / 2 ms bursts every 10 ms. No input capacitance on the NodeMCU board was assumed (conservative). AMS1117 minimum input = 3.3 V + 1.1 V dropout = 4.4 V.
- **Supply lead:** 20 mohm, 0.5 uH. Polyfuse: 0.25 ohm. C1 ESR 0.1 ohm. USB cable: 0.25 ohm. NodeMCU USB diode: modelled with the typical 1N5819 card (~0.35 V at 0.4 A).
- **Transistor model:** Philips/NXP BC337-40 Gummel-Poon card, as distributed in the LTspice libraries and in KiCad-Spice-Library BJT.lib (BF = 436.8). A copy with BF = 250 covers the hFE minimum. The model has no breakdown, so the BC337's V(BR)CES = 50 V is added as a separate breakdown diode (D_BVCES).
- **Diode models:** 1N4148 and 1N5819: the onsemi cards from the LTspice library (also in KiCad-Spice-Library DIODE2.lib). 1N5819_MAX: fitted here to the datasheet maximum, 0.60 V at 1 A. DI_1N5819: the Diodes Inc published model. Red LED: fitted to Vf ~1.95 V at 3 mA. All are in `models/`.
- **I2C:** the open-drain driver is a 60 ohm switch. Bus capacitance is a parameter; the real cable is not modelled.
- Simulations run at 27 C. Integration method: gear. Trapezoidal integration left an artificial, undamped L-C ring in the coil after release.
